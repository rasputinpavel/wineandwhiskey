# Intro Call Script — Restaurants & Bars (Phuket)

**Purpose:** First cold call to a HoReCa venue. Goal — book a 15–20 min in-person meeting. Not a tasting (tastings are coordinated through Bangkok).

**Memory anchors:** *~300 wines, 15+ countries* — we are a **wine shop**, not a one-country importer. Inside that: *Abrau Durso sparkling / Ladoga vodka / Barrister gin / indigenous reds*, the one range that is ours alone. Plus the two hooks that open doors by themselves — **a wine list at ฿300 cost-in** and **an event weekend where the venue risks nothing**.

**Leave-behind:** the **Partner Offer** deck (`partner_offer.pdf`) — all four offers in nine pages. Send it after the call, bring it printed to the meeting.

---

## Opening (10 sec)

Hi, this is [name] from Wine & Whiskey — we're the wine shop in Rawai. Around 300 wines from more than 15 countries, and we supply restaurants across the island. I'll be quick — just calling to introduce ourselves.

## Why we're calling (15 sec)

We're not asking you to switch your main supplier. We do four things for venues here — we can cover the whole wine list from one place, we hold the one range nobody else on the island imports, we rebuild wine cards so they earn more, and we run wine events inside the venue. Any one of them works on its own.

## What's in the cellar (30 sec)

— **The range** — France, Italy, Spain, Portugal, and the New World: Australia, USA, Argentina, Chile, New Zealand, South Africa. Champagne through to tawny port, plus natural, orange and pét-nat. From ฿400 house pours up to Pauillac Grand Cru. Whatever the card needs, it's one order to one number.

— **Spirits** — around sixty: whiskey, cognac, gin, rum, tequila. The name isn't decorative.

— **The exclusive bit** — we're the only importer on Phuket for a set of Russian houses: Abrau Durso sparkling (Prosecco alternative at the entry level, Crémant alternative a tier up, sharper price), Ladoga vodka across three tiers, Barrister gin — Bombay-plus quality at Beefeater-minus price — and reds from indigenous grapes like Krasnostop. Nobody else can put those on a table across the street from you.

## The wine-list angle (20 sec)

Separately from the labels — we also rebuild wine lists. We pick by what the bottle costs you, not by how famous the region is: less-known regions and indigenous grapes carry no price expectation, so the guest judges the glass. Bottles from ฿300 in, the same price on your menu as today, roughly double the margin. We write and design the printed card too — that part costs you nothing.

## The event angle (20 sec)

And the one most managers actually bite on. For two evenings we bring a mobile wine bar into your room — our wine on consignment, our stand, our sommelier. The guest orders from you and pays you: your till, your licence, your mark-up. You buy nothing up front, you pay only for what actually sold, and unsold bottles leave with us. If the evening flops, you owe us nothing.

## The Russian-tourist angle (15 sec)

One more thing worth mentioning — Phuket has a huge Russian guest flow. A familiar label on your list is often what closes the sale for them. That's a margin opportunity most venues here don't tap into.

## Close (10 sec)

Can I drop by for 15–20 minutes? I'll bring the catalog and pricing, and I can look at your current list and tell you what the margin difference would be. If something catches your eye, we'll coordinate samples as a next step. What works better — early week or end of week?

## Which hook to lead with

Managers hear "new supplier" ten times a month and "free event with no downside" almost never.

- **Venue that brushes off suppliers, or where the gatekeeper won't pass you up** — lead with the **event**. It isn't a purchase, so it isn't their boss's decision to block. Opening line: *"I'm not calling to sell you wine. We run wine evenings inside restaurants — our wine, our sommelier, you sell it and keep the mark-up, and you buy nothing."*
- **Venue with a visibly weak or expensive wine list** — lead with the **list**. *"Can I look at your wine list for ten minutes? I can usually find you the same menu price at half the cost-in."*
- **Venue that needs a whole card, or is juggling several suppliers** — lead with the **cellar**: 300 wines, 15+ countries, one delivery and one invoice.
- **Venue with Russian guests, or a Russian owner** — lead with the **exclusive labels** and the tourist flow.
- **Beach club, bar, cocktail-driven venue** — lead with **gin and vodka**, the list comes later.

## Cheat sheet — if they ask

- **"So you only do Russian wine?"** — No, the opposite. We're a wine shop first — about 300 wines in stock from more than 15 countries, Bordeaux and Barolo and Champagne and Catena, plus around sixty spirits. The Russian range is one shelf of that, and it happens to be the one nobody else on the island can supply.
- **"Why Russian?"** — Quality has come a long way. In sparkling and gin specifically, we offer the best price/quality on the island. Plus you get exclusivity — these labels aren't available through any other distributor here.
- **"We already have a wine supplier."** — Good, keep them. Two of the four things we do don't touch your supplier at all — an event weekend, and rebuilding the card. And on the wine itself we don't need to replace anyone: start with the Russian shelf, which nobody else can sell you anyway, and if the rest of the cellar turns out cheaper or easier, that decision makes itself later.
- **"Licensing / import?"** — Fully legal, official import, full paperwork. Happy to show documents when we meet.
- **"Send me the price list"** — Will do, and I'll send the partner offer with it — it explains the event format and the wine-list work, which the price list doesn't. But the price list alone won't tell you much, the labels are unfamiliar. A 15-minute meeting is more useful so I can show you which SKUs fit your concept.
- **"Can we taste first?"** — Tastings are coordinated through our Bangkok office, so I'd want to do them properly — pick the right SKUs for your venue first, then arrange a session.

### About the event

- **"What does the event cost us?"** — Printing the artwork we hand you. That's the entire list. Wine, stand, design and the sommelier are ours, and you pay only for bottles that actually sold.
- **"What if nothing sells?"** — Then you owe almost nothing. It's consignment — we settle after the weekend on what sold, there's no minimum, no shelf fee, no stock on your balance sheet. Unopened bottles leave with us free of charge.
- **"Who sells — you or us?"** — You. The sale goes through your till, in your venue, under your alcohol licence. We're the supplier. Our sommelier stands by the wine and does the talking and the pouring; your team just rings it up.
- **"How do the by-the-glass bottles work?"** — Inexpensive light bottles. The moment you open one for the glass you pay its cost price and the bottle is yours — at ฿160 a glass it pays for itself in about three pours, and you sell the rest at leisure, including after the event.
- **"Can we do it regularly?"** — Yes, it works best as a monthly weekend. Once guests know it happens, it becomes a reason to come back.

### About the wine list

- **"How can the wine be that cheap and still be good?"** — Because you stop paying for the region's reputation. A famous name puts ฿300 on the invoice before anything goes in the bottle. We buy where the money goes into the wine instead — and blind, those bottles win.
- **"Won't guests notice the names are unfamiliar?"** — That's the point. An unfamiliar label can't be price-checked on a phone, so the value is set by what's in the glass and by how the card describes it. We write those descriptions for you.
- **"Who pays for the printed card?"** — You print it, we design and write it. No design fee — we earn on the bottles.
