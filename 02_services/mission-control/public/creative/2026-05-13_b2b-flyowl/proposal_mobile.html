03 · Whites · what’s on your card today

The white page
leaves money on the table.

Today the white side is too short for the rooftop traffic, too Australian-heavy, and anchored on a wine nobody knows. Guests order whites first — especially with seafood and sashimi. Right now the easy pour is missing.

Audit · Whites
Your current list, line-by-line

What’s working

  • Ant Moore Sauvignon Blanc — solid New Zealand SB, keep it.
  • Penfold Bin 311 Chardonnay — quality bottle as the cherry on top, keep it.

What needs to change

  • Frontera Chardonnay — low quality, no name recognition. Guests pass.
  • Penfold Max’s & Koonunga Hill Chardonnay — same producer, two SKUs, guests confused. Pick one.
  • Penfold Bin 51 Riesling — too expensive for what Riesling is meant to do (light, quaffable).
  • Whole white side leans Australian — heavy bottles, hard to drink a second glass of.

The edit

–Remove Frontera Chardonnay, Penfolds Koonunga Hill Chardonnay, Penfold Bin 51 Riesling.
+Add a recognisable Italian glass tier (Pinot Grigio, Soave, Chardonnay) and a Mosel Riesling that’s actually fun to drink.
+Build a Chablis ladder — give the bottle page somewhere to climb. Pair Bin 311 with a real Burgundy alternative.

04 · Reds · what’s on your card today

The red page
needs a softer entry.

Your reds are all-Australian-shiraz heavy. Great muscle on the bottle, harder to drink a second glass of. The entry positions need lighter, friendlier names — and we’ve got one Chilean supplier (MontGras) that can replace three holes at once.

Audit · Reds
Your current list, line-by-line

What’s working

  • Penfold Bin 28 Shiraz — muscle position, keep it.

What needs to change

  • Frontera Cabernet Sauvignon — same problem as the white. Anonymous, low quality.
  • Edition Rouge Saint Hubert Pinot Noir — thin and rough. Replace with a real French Pinot.
  • Penfolds Max’s Cabernet — the high-check slot can do better than Penfolds at this price point.
  • No Malbec, no Montepulciano, no Merlot — the variety guests order by name is missing.

The edit

–Remove Frontera Cabernet, Edition Rouge Pinot Noir, Penfolds Max’s Cabernet.
+Add a MontGras anchor (Chile) covering three slots: entry Cabernet, soft Merlot, high-check Carmenère.
+Add French Pinot Noir, one Malbec, one Montepulciano, and a high-check American Cabernet.

05 · Sparkling · what’s on your card today

The sunset menu
is empty.

A Patong rooftop sells more sparkling than anything else at golden hour. Your card reads Chandon-or-Frontera — two SKUs, one of them anonymous. There’s no Prosecco ladder, no sparkling rosé for the spritz crowd, no champagne pair for the celebrations on the floor.

Audit · Sparkling
Your current list, line-by-line

What’s working

  • Chandon Brut — known name, keep it as the safe pour.
  • Cote de Provence Houchart Rosé — keep, but the still-rosé side is low-demand. One position is enough.

What needs to change

  • Only one mass-market sparkling next to a Frontera anchor — nothing for golden-hour ordering.
  • No sparkling rosé for the spritz crowd.
  • No off-dry / sweeter sparkling for guests who want it lighter.
  • One champagne in the cabinet, no pair.

The edit

+Add a three-step Prosecco ladder so you can pour from ฿530 to ฿610.
+Add a sparkling rosé pair: Lambrusco for the sweet side, Abrau Durso for the dry side.
+Add a champagne pair — one entry Brut, one Blanc de Noirs as the page anchor.
